What is Information Security?

Slide 2 / 17

The protection of information and system assets from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction to provide confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

Computer Security

Protecting individual automated computing devices.

Network Security

Securing data in transit across network channels.

Internet Security

Protecting browser transactions and web communications.

AI Model Security

Guarding ML models against data poisoning and privacy leakage.

🔍 Expand Deep-Dive & Advanced Concepts

Real-World Example: Imagine a banking database. Information Security ensures that hackers cannot view account balances (Confidentiality), alter account balances (Integrity), or take down the mobile app so users cannot withdraw money (Availability).

Prerequisites Needed: Data Communications, OSI Model, Network Protocols, Wireless Security, and AI Security Foundations.

The Security Requirement Triad (CIA)

Slide 3 / 17

Confidentiality

Preserving authorized restrictions on information access and disclosure. Protecting personal privacy and proprietary data.

Example: Encrypting electronic medical health records (EHR).

Integrity

Guarding against improper information modification or destruction. Ensures nonrepudiation and authenticity.

Example: Preventing unauthorized grade modifications in university portals.

Availability

Ensuring timely and reliable access to systems and information. Service is not denied to authorized users.

Example: Maintaining 99.999% uptime for emergency 911 dispatch networks.
🔍 Deep-Dive: Interdependence of CIA

Security is a balance. If you make a system 100% confidential by disconnecting it from the internet, you destroy its Availability. If you make it 100% available without passwords, you destroy Confidentiality.

Authenticity & Accountability

Slide 4 / 17

Authenticity

The property of being genuine and able to be verified and trusted. Ensures confidence in the validity of a transmission or message originator.

Tool: Digital Signatures & Multi-Factor Auth (MFA)

Accountability

The requirement that actions of an entity be traced uniquely to that entity. Supports non-repudiation, intrusion detection, and forensic fault isolation.

Tool: Audit Logs & SIEM Systems

Levels of Security Breaches

Slide 5 / 17

Low Impact Breach

Minor financial loss or slight degradation in capabilities. No serious harm to individuals.

Minor Damage

Moderate Impact Breach

Significant degradation in mission capability, notable asset damage, and financial loss.

Significant Harm

High Impact Breach

Severe or catastrophic loss of operations, major financial damage, or loss of human life.

Catastrophic Impact

Threats vs. Security Attacks

Slide 6 / 17

Threat (Potential)

A potential for violation of security. Exists when there is a circumstance, capability, or event that could breach security and cause harm.

Analogy: Leaving your house front door unlocked. The threat is that someone could walk in.

Attack (Active Act)

An assault on system security that derives from an intelligent threat. A deliberate attempt to evade security services.

Analogy: A burglar physically opening your unlocked door and stealing your valuables.

Passive Security Attacks

Slide 7 / 17

Passive attacks attempt to learn or make use of information from the system without affecting system resources (hard to detect).

Darth passively intercepts and listens to data packet contents without altering them.

1. Release of Message Contents

Reading sensitive or confidential emails and voice calls.

2. Traffic Analysis

Observing frequency and length of encrypted messages to infer communication patterns.

Active Security Attacks

Slide 8 / 17

Active attacks involve some modification of the data stream or the creation of a false stream.

Masquerade

Pretending to be a different authorized entity.

Replay Attack

Passive capture of data and retransmission to produce an unauthorized effect.

Modification

Altering valid message contents in transit.

Denial of Service

Disrupting server access for legitimate users.

Threats to Users & Identity Theft

Slide 9 / 17

Information Gathering Techniques

  • Shoulder Surfing: Looking over someone's shoulder to steal PINs/passwords.
  • Dumpster Diving: Rummaging through trash for physical papers and credentials.
  • Packet Sniffing: Capturing unencrypted network data packets.
  • Social Engineering: Tricking users into handing over secrets.

Tracking & Privacy Threats

  • Cookies: Files saved by browsers that can track browsing history.
  • Spyware: Hidden software that logs keystrokes and sensitive data.
  • Web Bugs / Beacons: Hidden pixels embedded in emails to track opening.
  • Spam: Unsolicited commercial email vectors.

Threats to Hardware & Physical Controls

Slide 10 / 17

1. Power Threats

Spikes, surges, and blackouts.

Countermeasure: UPS & Generators.

2. Theft & Vandalism

Physical theft of server drives or laptops.

Countermeasure: Cable Locks & Biometric Security.

3. Natural Disasters

Fires, floods, and earthquakes.

Countermeasure: Off-site Cloud Backups & DR Plans.

Threats to Data & Malicious Software

Slide 11 / 17

Malware Classifications

  • Virus: Self-replicating code attached to valid files.
  • Trojan Horse: Malicious program disguised as useful software.
  • DDoS Botnets: Networks of infected computers launching coordinated flooding attacks.

Essential Defense Tools

  • AES-256 Encryption at rest & in transit.
  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
  • Next-Gen Firewalls & VPN Tunnels.
